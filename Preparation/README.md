# Preparation

The measurements, curation and tools behind the randomizer. None of it ships
to players except the curated data; it is here so that the decisions can be
checked, and so that the next person - including a later version of the
author - can find out why something is the way it is.

Written 2026-08-30, when the whole folder was re-checked against the code.
Where a document contradicts the source, the source wins and the document is
the bug.

## The data chain, and what to re-run

This is the thing that is easiest to get wrong, because two of the four links
are not versioned and drift silently.

```
  TombItemEdit2026 -exportinfo        a separate program, NOT in this repo.
        |                             Found through the TR5_ITEMEDIT
        |                             environment variable.
        v
  Preparation\Level Infos\<level>.json     TRACKED. 16 files, one per level.
        |                                  Rooms, items, triggers and the
        |                                  sector grid - ZoneAnalysis reads
        |                                  all four, see below.
        v
  Preparation\Tools\ZoneAnalysis.ps1       the curation. Zones, key
        |                                  constraints, banned rooms and
        |                                  sectors, enemy types per level.
        v
  build\data\zones_<level>.json            NOT TRACKED, and regenerated by
        |                                  BuildRelease.ps1 for all 15 levels
        |                                  on every package build.
        v
  Preparation\Tools\EmbedData.ps1          bakes them, zlib-packed, into
        |                                  src\core\EmbeddedData.cpp - TRACKED,
        |                                  so the repository carries the data
        |                                  and the build needs no generator.
        v
  the randomizer carries them built in; a file in data\ beside the EXE
  still wins at run time (#142).
```

**Three rules follow from that, and all three have cost time before:**

1. **A change to ZoneAnalysis.ps1 does nothing until the exports are
   regenerated.** They are gitignored, so nothing warns you. Run
   `BuildRelease.ps1` (which does all fifteen - every level info except Title)
   or the script per level.
2. **The level infos are the measurement, not the curation.** Editing them
   by hand means lying to every tool downstream. If a level really changed,
   re-export it.
3. **The randomizer reads no level info at all any more.** Until 2026-08-30 a
   copy of these files shipped as `data\level_<name>.json`, 45 MB of it, and
   the run took three arrays out of them: rooms, items, triggers - 1.2% of the
   bytes. Those three now come out of the `.trc` the randomizer opens anyway
   (`src\core\LevelInfo.h`), proven level by level with
   `Tools\Diagnose\InfoCompare.exe` before the switch. What ships is the zone
   export and nothing else.

   The files here were shrunk to match: everything no tool reads is gone
   (`boxes`, `statics`, and eleven of the twenty-five sector fields), 51 MB
   down to 21 MB. The zone exports came out byte-identical afterwards, all
   fifteen of them, which is how that was checked rather than assumed.

4. **What is left is here because it cannot be computed.** `validForItems` is
   not a measurement but a curated judgement - a hand-written exclusion list
   and a reachability call sit among its ten conditions. Recomputing it would
   mean inventing it again. The other thirteen fields ZoneAnalysis reads could
   be derived from the `.trc`; they stay because PowerShell cannot read a
   `.trc`, so the win would be one file format more, not less.

## A standing warning about this folder

Every document here was written while something was being built, and several
were overtaken by the thing they described. The 2026-08-30 pass corrected
what was wrong and marked what had been overtaken rather than deleting it -
the reasoning is usually still worth reading even when the conclusion moved.

Two habits keep it honest:

- **Put the date on a measurement.** A number without one cannot be checked
  later, and it will be wrong eventually.
- **Name the symbol, not the line number.** Line numbers in `.cpp` files rot
  with the next edit. The trigger document has a whole paragraph about this,
  learned the hard way.
