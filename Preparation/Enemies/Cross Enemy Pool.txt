The cross-level enemy pool - what governs it

This document covers the larger feature: an enemy can be fetched out of
another level entirely, not only swapped for another WITHIN a level.

This file deliberately does NOT reproduce the pool table. That table is
TR5Randomizer\src\core\Randomizer.cpp (kCross and its neighbours), it changes
with the curation, and a copy here would be wrong within a fortnight. What
follows is what the table has to obey, and why - which is the part that does
not change and cannot be read off the numbers.


THE DONOR RULE
================================================================
A type may only be fetched from a level that has AT LEAST ONE VANILLA
INSTANCE of it. Not "a level whose file contains the model" - an instance.

The reason is sound. A level carries only the sound samples it needs itself.
A model can sit in a level file complete and still be mute there, because the
samples that belong to it were never packed.

Pierre is the proof, and the reason the rule is written down. His model lies
with 15 meshes in BOTH Andrea2 and Andrea3. He has an instance in NONE of
the thirteen levels, and his shot sample (SFX 90) is present in not a single
one. Imported anywhere, he shoots in silence.

So each pool row names a donor level, and each donor was chosen because the
enemy really stands in it.

The cyborg is the clean counter-example, and worth stating because it
shows what the rule buys. Only rich3 carries his model, and rich3 also holds
his two vanilla instances - so his samples are packed there, all six of them,
and every one hangs on an animation command. Phase D of the transfer picks
them up by itself, and his pool row needs no sound entry at all. Where the
rule is met, the sound question does not arise.


THE ANIMATION BASE
================================================================
Several soldier types carry only a ONE-FRAME STUB in the level files. The
engine bends their anim_index at runtime onto another type's set:

    SWAT, else guard 460, else guard 37

Whoever imports such a type must bring the base type along, or it runs
without a valid animation set - which does not crash. It goes invisible and
invulnerable, while its shadow and its damage stay. That is what makes it
expensive to debug and worth stating here.

The import brings the base with replace=false: if the target already has it,
natively or from its own cross import, it stays.

The base is the guard 460, not 37. 460 always carries
the clean joby3 set, while slot 37 may hold a different model per level - the
rich levels being the case that forced the change.


THE SWAP HALVES, AND WHY EVERY TYPE HAS HIS OWN SLOT
================================================================
Several enemies do not carry all their meshes in their own slot. The engine
copies a few of them at load time out of MESHSWAP1 (object slot 405) - fire
hands, damage skins, a swapped head. MESHSWAP1 is filled per level with the
content of THAT level's own enemies, so an imported enemy would draw whatever
the target happens to keep there, or, in the five levels without a 405, slot
0, which is Lara.

Each such type therefore has a fallback slot of his own, which the
randomizer fills from a level that really carries his set (the swapFrom and
swapDst columns of the pool table) and which the engine prefers over 405
(MeshSwapSource in setup.cpp, named MESHSWAP_CROSS_* in objects.h). Both
sides must agree on the number, which is why the assignment is written down
here and nowhere else:

    401 gladiator            462 SWAT            466 Mafia
    402 imp                  463 SWAT heavy      467 guard 460
    403 Roman god, sword     464 Two Gun         468 cyborg 469
    404 Roman god, hammer    465 guard 37        470 crane guy

What lands in such a slot is the donor's COMPLETE MESHSWAP1 - the copy does
not select, and the counts differ per donor (rich3 keeps 20 meshes there,
joby3 15). The engine then takes out the ones its type needs: the soldier
family 10 and 13, the imp 10, Two Gun 2, the gladiator 0 to 15, the Roman god
0 to 16, and the cyborg all twenty. He is the largest case in the project -
his damage skin is the whole model over again.

The crane guy (470) is a case that is easy to miss: his halves are only ever
shown at OCB 3, OCB 4 and SasControl state 37, while the pool places every
enemy with OCB 0.

Two donors here differ from the model donor, and for the same reason both
times - the model level has no MESHSWAP1 at all: the guard's hands come from
joby3 although his model comes from rich1, and the crane guy's come from
joby3 although his model comes from joby2.

The halves are copied with onlyMeshes: MESHSWAP1 carries an anim_index
without animations of its own, and only the meshes are wanted.


REACHING PAST THE OWN SLICE
================================================================
An object slot "owns" its animations up to the next object. Some control
routines reach past that boundary into the global anim table with
anim_index + N. If N is larger than the slice, the import's heuristic cuts
too early and the engine reads past the end of the table.

The pool therefore carries an explicit anim slice length for such types
(animCount). So far only the Imp needs it - and the reason is instructive:
andy1's Imp set is gutted, its death animation is a one-frame dummy without a
KillItem command, so the donor must be andy3.

The tool for this is SlotAnims (Preparation\Tools\Diagnose, bsa.bat).


PLACING
================================================================
Four small tables decide what a placed enemy needs:

  kSpaceNeeds   how much free room. The Hydra (63) needs a 3x3 window
                around her with at least 5 of its 9 tiles free (a
                stricter threshold of all nine tiles throws away 93
                percent of the spots).
  kDrawWeights  how many tickets a kind holds in the roll. Only the Hydra
                (63), with 2. Each roll picks
                uniformly among the kinds that fit at that very spot, so a
                kind the space rule keeps off most spots stays rare - at
                weight 1 she measured 4.2 a run against 32 to 64
                Compsognathi (9.1 at weight 2, see Enemy Pools.txt). The
                weight only makes her win more often where she already
                qualified; it opens no new place, and a kind with weight 1
                keeps its single entry and its order.
  kHerds        which types are placed as a group. Only the Compsognathus
                (53), four of them - one Compy alone is not the animal.
  kSpawnOcb     which types get a behaviour OCB set on purpose. The Roman
                god sword (61) gets 1 outside Andrea3, the hammer version
                (461) gets 0, the chef (87) gets 1 so he hunts.

Everything else is set to OCB 0 unless its type stands in KEEP_OCB - see
Item Infos\Item Pool IDs.txt for that rule and its exceptions.


THE FOUR GUESTS FROM OUTSIDE
================================================================
T-Rex, raptor, Compsognathus and vulture do not come from a TR5 level at all.
They are read at run time from the player's own Tomb Raider III installation
(the vulture from TR3 Gold, which is configured separately) and are marked in
the pool as coming from a foreign source. Without those installations they
simply do not appear; nothing else is affected.

Which files, and their checksums: Preparation\Tr3Checksums.csv.


BEFORE ADDING A TYPE TO THE POOL
================================================================
Four checks, each of which has caught something:

1. Does it have a vanilla instance in the donor level? (the donor rule)
2. Does its control read item->hit_points? If not, the enemy is IMMORTAL.
   Vanilla hides this because the levels concerned disarm Lara or end
   immediately after; a randomizer that scatters weapons does not. Three
   were found this way - chef, skeleton, swamp witch. One grep in the
   enemy's own .cpp answers it.

   There is a SECOND road to the same place, and grepping for hit_points
   does not find it: objects[...].undead in setup.cpp. The flag makes
   larafire.cpp skip the damage entirely (the grenade checks it too), so
   the control may read hit_points as diligently as it likes - nothing ever
   subtracts from them. The cyborg is the case: in vanilla he dies
   only through rich3's own geometry, its gas rooms and a short circuit in
   water, neither of which exists in another level. Two enemies carried the
   flag; Two Gun's was removed and confirmed in game, and for the cyborg
   the mortal version got an engine slot of its own so rich3's puzzle
   carriers keep theirs. So the check is two greps, not one: hit_points in
   the control, and undead in setup.cpp.
3. Does it need AI markers (Pool A) or not (Pool B)? See Enemy Pools.txt.
4. Does it hang on a level script or a cutscene? The chef did, through
   deltapak.cpp, which is why he was excluded. The other
   functions in that file have never been gone through the same way.
