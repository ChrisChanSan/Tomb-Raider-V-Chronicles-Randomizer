TOMB5 (TR5/Chronicles) - enemy AI pool for the randomizer
=============================================================
Determined from the TOMB5 engine source code
(TOMB5 Code\TOMB5\game\*.cpp)

Background
----------
TR5 levels place invisible AI marker objects (AIPatrol1, AIAmbush,
...) at certain spots. An enemy item gets an `ai_bits` flag
(GUARD/PATROL1/AMBUSH/FOLLOW/MODIFY) when placed in the editor. Only
if the enemy's object kind calls the shared engine function
GetAITarget() (game/box.cpp) does the enemy actually look for such a
marker and walk to it, resp. fire a trigger on ambush as soon as it
reaches it. Object kinds that never call GetAITarget() ignore every
AI marker completely and pursue Lara instead via a generic "mood"
system (CreatureAIInfo/GetCreatureMood/CreatureMood) or hard-wired
script logic.

For the randomizer this means: enemies from Pool A can safely be
mixed onto patrol/ambush spawn points, enemies from Pool B need their
own spawns without marker dependency (otherwise the intended
patrol/ambush behavior is simply ignored - no crash, just
wrong/missing behavior).

Special case - no placeable single enemies, but FX swarm emitters
---------------------------------------------------------------------
RAT, BAT, SPIDER: TriggerRat/ControlBatEmitter/TriggerSpider spawn
particle swarms from a fixed pool (rat.cpp/bat.cpp/spider.cpp).
No obj->intelligent, no CreatureCollision, no AI system involved.
Generally not relevant for the position pool logic - they are not
treated like normal enemy items.

A related topic, investigated separately: a pickup placed too close
to an enemy (512 units X/Z, 256 Y) is collected as his carried_item via
GetCarriedItems() (game/setup.cpp) and floor-dropped after his death.
On the drop (DropBaddyPickups, game\box.cpp) the engine clears the low
OCB bits of the wall pickups, OCB 2 and 9, so those land as ordinary
floor pickups. Every other OCB is kept as it is: a hidden pickup (OCB 1)
dropped that way keeps its hidden-pickup manner, but a plinth pickup
(OCB 3, 4, 7, 8) dropped that way stays permanently uncollectable -
FindPlinth (game\pickup.cpp) finds no plinth at the drop spot.

For the randomizer the rule is "normalize everything to OCB 0", with two
exception tables, and both matter.
  KEEP_OCB keeps the OCB for wall pickups, where it encodes the pickup
  STYLE rather than a leftover of the old spot (Andrea2 and andy3 items
  172 and 174: OCB 2 is the crowbar grip, OCB 9 the fence). Set to 0
  they would not be pickable at all. The table lives in
  Preparation\Tools\ZoneAnalysis.ps1 and reaches the randomizer through
  the zone export as keepOcbTypes.
  kSpawnOcb goes the other way and SETS an OCB on purpose, to choose an
  enemy's behaviour: the Roman god sword (61) gets OCB 1 outside
  Andrea3, the hammer version (461) gets 0, and the chef (87) gets 1 so
  he hunts instead of chopping vegetables.
Both are in TR5Randomizer\src\core\Randomizer.cpp.
