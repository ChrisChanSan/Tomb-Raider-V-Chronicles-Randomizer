Which items the randomizer may move, and what governs each kind.

The three categories below live in code and curation tables, not in this
file. This file says where each one lives.


================================================================
1. Generic items - anywhere, no logic
================================================================
Weapons, ammunition, medipacks, flares. Freely placeable.

	349 = Bigmedi Item (large medipack)
	350 = Smallmedi Item (small medipack)
	348 = Revolver Ammo Item
	355 = Flare Inv Item ("Flares") - THIS is the collectable pickup
	354 = Flare Item - do NOT place. That is the flare Lara throws away
	      once it is used up, a throw/deco object with no pickup logic.
	      trview's type column shows the difference: "Flare" vs "Flares".

THE OCB RULE. A moved item does NOT always get OCB 0, and the exception
matters.

	A moved item gets OCB 0 only if its type is NOT in keepOcbTypes. The
	KEEP_OCB table (Preparation\Tools\ZoneAnalysis.ps1, reaching the
	randomizer through the zone export) lists the types whose OCB encodes
	the pickup STYLE rather than a leftover of the old spot: the wall
	pickups with OCB 2 (crowbar grip) and OCB 9 (fence) in Andrea2 and
	andy3. Zeroed, those cannot be picked up at all. See #61.

	A warning worth heeding: this rule applies only to real pickup
	objects. On an enemy item the OCB means
	something else entirely - the Hydra's chain position, for instance.
	Always filter by object_number first.

	Carried pickups: an item placed within 512 units X/Z and 256 Y of an
	enemy is picked up by GetCarriedItems() (game\setup.cpp) as that
	enemy's carried_item and dropped on its death. DropBaddyPickups
	(game\box.cpp) clears the low OCB bits of a wall pickup (OCB 2 or 9)
	on the drop - the case behind that was an Imp carrying a crowbar away
	in joby3. In game the clear is not enough: such a pickup is either
	gone or lies at a wrong position, still with its wall-pickup identity,
	and cannot be collected (observed in a play test; the cause is
	not measured yet), so wall pickups are kept away from enemies. Every other
	OCB is kept, so a hidden pickup (OCB 1) dropped that way keeps its
	hidden-pickup manner, but a plinth pickup (OCB 3, 4, 7 or 8) dropped
	that way stays permanently uncollectable - FindPlinth (game\pickup.cpp)
	finds no plinth at the drop spot.


================================================================
2. Key and puzzle items - must watch rooms
================================================================
NOT an open TODO. This is a full per-level curation, finished for all
thirteen levels, and it is far too large to keep in a text file.

	The curation lives in Preparation\Tools\ZoneAnalysis.ps1 and is
	written out per level as keyConstraints in the zone export
	(build\data\zones_<level>.json). The randomizer reads it into struct
	KeyConstraint, whose fields are the vocabulary of the whole system:

	  typeId            which item this rule is about
	  maxZone           how far into the level it may travel
	  allowRooms        rooms it may use at all
	  excludeRooms      rooms it may not
	  islandSectors     a defined area it must stay inside
	  bannedSectors     single tiles that are out
	  forbiddenSectors  tiles barred for every key item
	  requireInIsland   whether the island is mandatory or preferred

	Changing any of it means re-generating the zone exports - see the note
	on that in ZoneAnalysis.ps1 itself. The exports are not versioned.


================================================================
3. Items that fire triggers
================================================================
NOT a hand-written list either, and deliberately so: the set is derived from
the level data on every run.

	findTriggerPinnedItems reads each trigger's reference word out of the
	level file and pins the items a trigger points at, so an item that
	opens a door cannot wander away from it. Because it is derived, a
	curation change or a new level needs no maintenance here.

	Two deliberate departures from a blanket exclusion:
	  - a pickup trigger whose only further effect is PlaySoundtrack does
	    not pin its item; music is not a dependency.
	  - neither does one that only spawns despawning swarm critters
	    (bat, rat, spider - types 93 to 96).
	Where a trigger genuinely must travel with its item, LevelFix.cpp
	rewrites it rather than pinning the item in place.

	The example this file was built around, resolved from the repo's own
	export (Level Infos\rich1.json):
	  rich1 item 14 = PICKUP_ITEM2 (221), room 11, OCB 8, sector (5,5) -
	  the Iris. The pickup trigger on that sector fires items 14, 15 and
	  206:
	    item 14  = itself, i.e. the deactivation
	    item 15  = typeId 156, XRAY_CONTROLLER, room 11
	    item 206 = typeId 304, CLOSED_DOOR3, room 155 - the gate from
	               zone 4 to zone 5.
