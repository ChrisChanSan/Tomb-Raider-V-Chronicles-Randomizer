Outfits - what travels, what stays, and why

Written 2026-08-30. The wardrobe and the guest outfits from Tomb Raider III
have shipped for weeks; Preparation mentioned them twice, in passing, in
files about something else. This is the record.

The tables live in TR5Randomizer\src\core\Tr3Source.cpp (outfits()) and the
transfer in ApplyPatch.cpp. As with the enemy pool, what follows is the part
that does not change - the rules and the reasons.


WHAT AN OUTFIT IS, IN FILE TERMS
================================================================
Three object slots move: 8 LARA_SKIN, 9 LARA_SKIN_JOINTS, 10 LARA_SCREAM.
Plus one byte, the LaraType (below).

NEVER slot 0. Slot 0 carries the LEVEL'S OWN ANIMATIONS, not Lara's looks.
Taking it along was tried and the result was seen in game: the switch and
crawl animations went missing, because the donor level did not have the ones
the target level needs.

There is no half outfit. If one of the three slots fails, the patch aborts
BEFORE saving and the level stays the untouched copy from the backup.


THE HEAD STAYS THE TARGET LEVEL'S OWN
================================================================
This looks like a shortcut and is the opposite. The engine rewrites
lara.mesh_ptrs[LM_HEAD] at run time - the scream head, the speaking heads in
cutscenes - and the braid takes its anchor and its collision radius from
whatever mesh sits in that slot.

Keeping the target level's head therefore keeps six heads and the braid
correct for free. Bringing the donor's head would mean rebuilding all of
that, for a difference nobody sees under a hood.


THE LaraType BYTE
================================================================
Core hung the braid's special handling off the LEVEL NAME, because in vanilla
an outfit never crosses a section. As soon as it does, that breaks: rich1's
braid stuck to the cap's head vertex numbers, seen in game.

The randomizer therefore writes a LaraType byte into the level as an outfit
marker, and hair.cpp and laraskin.cpp key off it instead - both the anchoring
and the weld table of the braid base. The value is decided in the randomizer
(2 = Joby outfit anywhere, 5 = Andrea outfit in a Russia level); a
hand-written patch without the field falls back to the old rule.

This is one of the couplings in Contract.md: the byte is written by one tree
and read by the other, and nothing checks that they agree.


THE FOUR GUESTS FROM TOMB RAIDER III
================================================================
London, Jungle, Antarctica and Nevada. All four come from the player's own
Tomb Raider III installation, read at run time - no game data ships here.
Their source levels and checksums are in Preparation\Tr3Checksums.csv;
ROOFS, CRASH, ANTARC and NEVADA, object 315 in each.

Two things about them that cost time:

  Object 315, not object 0. In TR1-3 object 0 is a placeholder; the real
  model is "Lara true appearance".

  The skin has to be recoloured. TR3's flat skin tones and TR5's 5-bit
  levels look identical as numbers and are not: 224 falls on level 28 and
  comes back as 231, while TR5's own arm sits on 27 and comes back as 222.
  That one step is a visible seam wherever imported skin meets the head -
  which is kept from the target level, see above. The recolour tables are
  per outfit, in Tr3Source.cpp.


BEFORE ADDING AN OUTFIT
================================================================
1. Does the donor level really carry all three slots?
2. Is the model object 315, not 0?
3. Does the skin need a recolour against the head that will stay?
4. Which LaraType does it need, and does the braid sit right in a level of
   the OTHER section? That is the case the byte exists for, and the only way
   to know is to look.
